# Wildcamp CH

Tap a spot on the map and see whether wild camping there is likely allowed in Switzerland, and how well you would sleep. The first view is one overall score with its reason and the dangers that matter; everything else is one tap away. It is guidance, not legal advice: verdicts are `Not allowed`, `Caution`, `Likely OK` or `Unknown`, and the app never says "legal".

## What it checks

### Legality

- **Federal zones** via the geo.admin.ch `identify` API (`src/zones.ts`, `src/geoadmin.ts`). What each does, from the primary texts saved in `docs/sources/CH/`:
   - *Not allowed*: Swiss National Park; federal hunting reserves (VEJ Art. 5 para. 1 let. e bans free tenting and camping); wildlife quiet zones whose own data says statutory entry ban or path-only rule **and** whose season is running today.
   - *Caution* (no federal camping ban by name, so local rules decide): floodplains and glacier forefields (AuenV Art. 5 para. 2 let. c), raised bogs, fens, dry meadows, amphibian sites, waterbird reserves, Pro Natura reserves, and wildlife quiet zones that are only recommended, have another rule, apply to winter sports only, or are out of season today. An army shooting zone becomes caution on a day when shooting is listed.
   - *Info*: mire landscapes, BLN landscapes, army shooting zones with no firing today, and glacier ice (hazard note, not a legal finding).
   - The federal wildlife-quiet-zone map is incomplete (BAFU: status varies between cantons), so a ridge can be in a zone that is not on it. The result says so.
- **Cantonal nature reserves** (Bern, Ticino, Valais, Geneva, Glarus, Fribourg, Lucerne, Solothurn and Jura so far): Bern's 244 reserves are bundled as polygons (`public/reserves-be.json.gz`, 132 KB). Each reserve's protection decree was downloaded (scans OCR'd) and searched for camping clauses, in German and French: 181 name camping or tenting as prohibited and 20 prohibit entering the reserve or leaving the marked paths, so camping is effectively banned (all 201 → "Not allowed", with the decree link); the other 43 are old decrees that say nothing about camping, so they are "Caution". The scan is automated and checked by reading the matches for every reserve that was not obvious, not by reading every decree line by line; see `docs/sources/BE/reserves_decree_scan.csv`. Ticino's 351 in-force protection-decree polygons (`public/reserves-ti.json.gz`, 63 KB) come from the canton's WFS; the 26 decrees behind them were read in Italian: 15 prohibit camping (294 polygons → "Not allowed", with the decree link and any stated exception such as authorised occasional-camping areas), 11 say nothing (57 polygons → "Caution"); see `docs/sources/TI/reserves_decree_scan.csv`. Valais's 100 sites protected by cantonal decision (`public/reserves-vs.json.gz`, ArcGIS layer `Nature/1101`) link to 49 decisions on `lex.vs.ch`, read in French: 12 prohibit camping or tenting and 3 prohibit entering the marsh (or leaving the paths), so 15 of the 49 decisions are "Not allowed"; the other 34 decisions list prohibited activities without camping and stay "Caution"; see `docs/sources/VS/reserves_decision_scan.csv`. Jura reserves are queried live from the canton's WMS and tested against the returned polygon; the canton states overnighting outside campsites is banned in all its nature reserves. Geneva's 68 sites (`reserves-ge.json.gz`): the cantonal regulation on nature reserves prohibits all activity except on signed paths, so 'reserves' are "Not allowed" and other protected sites "Caution". Lucerne's 20 protected areas (`reserves-lu.json.gz`): 18 protection ordinances on `srl.lu.ch` prohibit camping/tenting or entering ("Not allowed"), 2 are silent ("Caution"); see `docs/sources/LU/reserves_ordinance_scan.csv`. Glarus's protected areas (`reserves-gl.json.gz`) and Fribourg's 18 reserves (`reserves-fr.json.gz`) are only "Caution" because their camping rules were not read. All of these are built by `scripts/build_*_reserves.py`. Solothurn's 101 reserves (`reserves-so.json.gz`): the in-force legal documents (143 PDFs, scans OCR'd) were classified like Bern's; 11 prohibit camping ("Not allowed"), 90 do not name it or have no readable document ("Caution"); see `docs/sources/SO/reserves_document_scan.csv`. The other 16 cantons were searched and nothing usable was found (no published polygons, services that block scripted access, or polygons without legal texts): AG, AI, AR, BL, BS, GR, NE, NW, OW, SG, SH, SZ, TG, UR, VD, ZG, ZH. Details in `docs/CANTON_RESEARCH.md`.
- **Forest / treeline**: a forest map built from swissTLM3D forest polygons (`public/forest-mask.bin.gz`, 25 m grid, 3.7 MB, loaded by the browser at startup) combined with elevation. See `src/treeline.ts`:
   - in forest (closed, open, or shrub forest) → `forest`
   - otherwise compare elevation with the **local upper forest limit** (`public/treeline-surface.bin.gz`, 1 km grid, 29 KB): ≥ limit + 100 m → `above`; < limit − 100 m → `below`; within ±100 m → `unknown`
   - where there is no local estimate (no forest within 5 km): fixed bands — below 1500 m → `below`; forest within 500 m → `unknown`; ≥ 1800 m → `above`; else `unknown`

   The local limit comes from the swissTLM3D forest polygons' vertex heights: for each 500 m cell, the 98th percentile of the forest vertices within 1.5 km (widening to 3 km and then 5 km where there is hardly any forest). An earlier version took the maximum over 5 km, which let one high stand of trees in the next valley decide: near Capanna Barone (Ticino) it read 2177 m while forest within 1.5 km ends near 1800 m, so treeless ground at 2050 m was called "below the treeline". "Above" is never claimed below 1600 m, and nothing below 1500 m counts as above the treeline.

   If the forest map fails to load, it falls back to an elevation-only estimate and says so.
- **Canton** (swissBOUNDARIES3D) shown for every spot, with its camping rule where one has been read from the primary text. Currently recorded: OW, NW, TI, VD, SG, AI (6 of 26) (Ticino: restricted, but tolerated above the treeline, since the law excepts tenting for a bivouac in the mountains and does not define the term); the others show "cantonal rules are not verified". A `banned` rule forces "Not allowed" and `restricted` caps at "Caution". Rules live in `src/cantons.ts`; tests require an official cantonal/federal source, a check date and the saved source text under `docs/sources/`. See `docs/CANTON_RESEARCH.md`.
- **Municipality** (swissBOUNDARIES3D) is shown for every spot. Municipal rules are recorded only where the municipality's own regulation was read (`src/municipalities.ts`, texts under `docs/sources/municipal/`), 43 rules so far (15 "Not allowed", 28 capped at "Caution"). *Camping banned on all land or all freely accessible land ("Not allowed")*: Kandersteg, Lauterbrunnen, Grindelwald, Wilderswil, Brienz, Lauenen, Gsteig (Bern); Andermatt, Göschenen (Uri); Davos, Klosters, Pontresina, St. Moritz, Flims (Graubünden). *Public ground only, or unclear (capped at "Caution")*: City of Bern, Interlaken, Spiez, Reichenbach, Adelboden, Lenk, Saanen, Fiesch, Fieschertal, Leukerbad, Arosa, Saas-Fee, and Realp (its copy of the ordinance lacks the approval date). Read with no camping ban: Meiringen, Riederalp, Grächen, Wildhaus-Alt St. Johann. Nothing found on the municipality's own pages: Hasliberg, Innertkirchen, Guttannen, Frutigen, Muotathal; not readable: Bettmeralp, and Zermatt, whose regulation sits behind a bot check and is blocked from this environment (it is shown as an unverified press report, capping the verdict at "Be careful", never as a ban); Glarus Süd's bans (Panixerpass, Oberblegisee, Muttenchopf) are reported as owner or court bans, not a regulation, and are shown as an unverified press report. *Cities (read 2026-10-04, see `docs/CANTON_RESEARCH.md`)*: Locarno bans camping on all municipal public ground and on private land open to the public ("Not allowed"); Zürich, Winterthur, St. Gallen, Aarau, Biel, Solothurn, Schaffhausen, Köniz, Chur, Thun, Lausanne, Fribourg, Neuchâtel, Sion and Zug (public facilities, including lakeshore facilities) regulate it on public ground (capped at "Caution"). Basel, Luzern, Bellinzona, Geneva and Lugano were read and have no general camping rule (only at named lake sites or in parks), so none is recorded. Everywhere else the app tells users to look up the municipality's police regulation. Entries use the same source gate as cantonal rules (official host, check date, saved text).
- **Villages and cities** (`src/buildingzone.ts`): the federal texts were read (saved in `docs/sources/CH/`, summary in `README_settlements.md`). No federal law bans or permits camping in built-up areas, but three provisions decide what it means there: Art. 699 ZGB gives everyone access to **forest and pasture** only (so not to gardens, fields, yards or built-up land), Art. 641 ZGB lets an owner repel unjustified use, and Art. 186 StGB punishes, on complaint, staying against the owner's will in a house or in the fenced yard or garden belonging to it. The app therefore looks the spot up in the national harmonised building zones (ARE, `ch.are.bauzonen`): *inside a building zone* the verdict is capped at "Be careful" with those reasons and the zone category; *within about 150 m of one* a "Close to a village or city" note is added without changing the verdict; a failed lookup is stated. It never says "Not allowed" by itself, because that needs a rule read at the source: those are the municipal police regulations, recorded per municipality (`src/municipalities.ts`).
- **Season calendar** (`src/seasons.ts`, `src/seasonview.ts`): for every wildlife quiet zone with a protection season in the source data ("15.12.-30.04."), the legal details show a 12-month strip (dark = restricted, half = part of the month, outlined = now) and when the restriction next starts or is lifted. Only wildlife quiet zones carry a season in the data; hunting-ban areas, reserves and parks are year-round, and alp grazing or similar local closures are not in any national dataset, so they are not shown.
- **Report a rule**: every result ends with "Report a missing or wrong rule", a link that opens a prefilled GitHub issue (place, link to the spot, what the app said, a request for the official source). Nothing is sent from the app; the reporter reviews the issue first. A reported rule is added only after its text has been read at the municipality's or canton's own page, saved under `docs/sources/` and covered by a test, the same bar as every other rule. *Unverified notes* (`UNVERIFIED_NOTES` in `src/municipalities.ts`) are the one exception to "read it yourself": a rule reported by press or guides whose text could not be read is shown as a warning with its sources and caps the verdict at "Be careful", never "Not allowed". Only Zermatt has one so far.

### Sleep comfort and weather

- **Sleep comfort** (separate from legality; not shown where camping is not allowed or outside Switzerland; follows the night chosen in the weather card). Code in `src/comfort/`. Rule-of-thumb rating (great / good / okay / poor) from:
   - *Terrain*, from the swisstopo elevation model through the geo.admin.ch profile service (2 m model, four lines through the spot, 10 m spacing within 500 m and 50 m spacing within 5 km): slope over 20 m (can you pitch a tent), wind shelter (average horizon angle around the spot, and whether it is an open ridge or top), hollows (cold air), steep slopes above and drops close by.
   - *Weather for the chosen night* (evening 18:00 to 08:00; pick tonight or one of the next three nights) from Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0, hourly, adjusted to the spot's elevation). It changes the rating: lowest temperature, gusts compared with the terrain horizon on the windward side, rain amount and chance, snow and freezing level, fog or condensation, and thunderstorms. A forecast thunderstorm or gusts of 80 km/h or more cap the night at "Poor" however good the spot is. The card shows the spot alone and the weather's effect separately.
   - *Crowds*, judged from distances only (no visitor counts exist in these datasets): marked hiking trails, huts and inns, public transport and cableway stops, car parks and villages.
   - *Water* in the swissTLM3D hydrography within 400 m, and *morning sun* (sun position against the terrain horizon).
   The factor thresholds and the rating cut-offs are my own judgement and have not been tested against campers' experience. Trees, rock, snow and local wind effects are not modelled. A check that could not run is listed as missing and never counted as good.
- **Weather card** (every spot in Switzerland): a night picker (tonight and the next three nights), headline tiles (sky, low and freezing level, gusts and direction, rain and chance), and three hourly charts (temperature, rain, wind gusts) with hover and keyboard readouts and a table view. Code: `src/weatherview.ts`, `src/chart.ts`, `src/comfort/weather.ts`. The forecast request asks for the extra variables (rain chance, sky code, cloud, snow, freezing level, dew point) and falls back to the core five if the service refuses them.
- **Scores and details**: the first view is one overall score; its details open three cards, *Legality*, *Sleep* and *Weather*, each with a 0 to 100 score and a label, and tapping a card opens its details (the legality checklist with sources, the sleep factors, the forecast). The sleep details start with chips for the nearest water (with glacier-water and dirty-water tags) and the nearest hut. The scores are plain summaries, not probabilities (`src/scores.ts`): *legality* is 0 for not allowed, 85 for likely OK (never higher: rules and private land are never fully checked), 40 for unknown, and for caution 55 minus 10 per warning (at least 25); *sleep* is 50 plus 6.25 per point of the comfort factor total, capped at 25 in a storm. Outside Switzerland there is no score.
- **Weather score**: the Weather card (`weatherScore` in `src/scores.ts`) rates the forecast for the chosen night alone: 50 is neutral, 12.5 points per factor point, a thunderstorm or storm-force gusts cap it at 10. Its details hold the forecast charts and the factors that come from the forecast (wind against the open directions, cold, rain, snow, fog); the Sleep details keep only the spot's own factors. The Sleep score still includes the weather, so a storm still pulls it down.
- **Water** (a main sleep criterion, `src/comfort/water.ts`): the nearest stream or lake in the swissTLM3D hydrography within 800 m (types 4 and 101 are read as streams and lakes; other types are ignored because I could not find the object catalogue to confirm what they are). Close water scores up (about 150 m: +2, 400 m: +1), no water within 800 m scores down, and water right beside the spot is flagged as noisy and flood-prone. *Glacier water*: glacier ice within 1 km of the water (swisstopo glacier extent) is tagged "glacier water", within 3 km "maybe glacier water"; this is judged from distance only, so a large river fed by a glacier far upstream is not tagged, and a nearby hanging glacier can tag a stream it does not feed. *Dirty water*: only one kind is known from the data, a wastewater treatment plant on the same watercourse (same watercourse number) that lies higher than the water, from the federal plant register; it is tagged "dirty: sewage upstream" with the plant and its share of the river's low flow. Livestock, wildlife and unmapped sources are not known, so every result says to treat or filter surface water. When no water is mapped within reach (or the water is far), a spring recorded on the geological map (swissGEOCOVER2D, kind "Quelle", `ch.swisstopo.geologie-swissgeocover2d_points`) within the search radius is named, with whether it is captured. The map is patchy: a spring found is real, one not found proves nothing, and it does not say the water is drinkable or flowing. There is no federal drinking-water data, so the app never calls any water safe (`docs/sources/CH/README_springs.md`).
- **Shelter nearby** (`src/comfort/shelters.ts`): club huts (SAC, CAS, CAI, CAF), other huts and rifugi, bivouac boxes, mountain inns and named alps within 5 km, from swissNAMES3D by name and object type. A chip shows the nearest hut with its distance and the sleep details list the rest; a hut within 1.5 km adds a point, and the thunderstorm and storm-gust warnings name the nearest hut. Distances are straight lines, not walking times, and the app cannot know opening times or whether a winter room is open, so it says so. Alp buildings are described as usually private and locked outside the summer season, so not a dependable emergency shelter. Only what swissNAMES3D maps is found: an unnamed or unmapped hut will be missing. For a club hut that swisstopo's winter-accommodation layer (`ch.swisstopo.unterkuenfte-winter`, made with the SAC) lists, the sleep details link to its SAC portal page, where the opening months, phone and beds are (329 of the layer's 506 points have a link; a summer-only hut is not in it, which does not mean it is closed). Only links to `sac-cas.ch` are used (`src/comfort/hutlink.ts`).
- **Show on map** tapping the water or hut chip (marked "📍 map"), or the "Show on map" button under a water or hut detail, flies the map to that place and marks it with an orange dot and its name, keeping your spot in view above or beside the sheet. The position is the point of the lake, stream or building outline closest to your spot (LV95, converted back with `lv95ToWgs84`, accurate to about a metre). Tapping elsewhere clears it.
- **Ground cover** (`src/comfort/ground.ts`): the sleep score counts what the tent sits on, from the Federal Statistical Office's land-cover statistics (`ch.bfs.arealstatistik-bodenbedeckung`, NOLC04, 27 categories, latest survey record). Ground is one of the heaviest factors: grass and herb vegetation scores +2 (soft, pegs hold); shrubs and brush meadows 0; stony ground (scree, gravel) -2; solid rock -3 (hard, no pegs); wetland and open water -2; ice and firn -4. Grass against rock is a 31 point swing on the 0 to 100 sleep score. The statistics are visual readings of aerial photos at the points of a 100 m grid, so the class comes from the nearest grid point (at most about 70 m away) and the app says so when it is more than 50 m off. A small lawn or a boulder field narrower than the grid is not seen. Land-cover names are shown in the language of the page.
- **Sleeping near huts, inns and alps** within 300 m of a club hut, mountain inn/restaurant or alp, the legality details show a warning and "Probably allowed" becomes "Caution". Source: the SAC leaflet on camping and bivouacking (2014, saved under `docs/sources/CH/`): do not sleep too close to huts, ask the hut team and offer to pay for the toilet or buy something, ask the farmer or hut team near alp and mountain huts. It is guidance, not law, and names no distance (300 m is this app's choice). The legality details show the three most important items and the rest behind "Show all N details".
- **Sun and moon** (`src/comfort/moon.ts`): *Evening sun*: besides the morning sun, the sleep details say how long the sun stays on the spot before sunset once the terrain horizon is applied: to sunset +1, a long shaded evening (the terrain hides the sun two hours or more before sunset) as information. *Moon*: phase, how much of the disc is lit and when the moon is up during the chosen night, from `suncalc`'s moon model (checked against the full moon of 7 Oct 2025 and the new moon of 21 Oct 2025); shown in the Weather details as bright moonlight, dark sky or partial moon. The moon never changes the score, since bright moonlight helps some campers and bothers others, and the terrain horizon is ignored for it. *Sunset and darkness*: the bearing of sunset and sunrise (so the tent door can face the right way), the start of the warm low light (sun 6 degrees above a flat horizon) and when the sky is fully dark (sun more than 18 degrees below it), for the chosen night. The sun times and bearings were checked against the Python library `astral` (bearings within 0.4 degrees, twilight within 2 minutes, `tests/comfort.test.ts`); the numbers are for a flat horizon and say so, because mountains make sunset earlier and sunrise later (the terrain-aware morning and evening sun items above account for that).
- **Ground frost** on top of the existing hollow, low-temperature and fog factors, a clear (cloud 60 % or less), calm night can leave the ground and tent colder than the forecast air at 2 m. The estimate takes 3 degrees off for clear sky (1.5 for partly clear) scaled by calm air, and 1 or 2 more in a hollow (cold air collects there); if the result reaches 0 °C while the forecast low is still above 0 (up to 8 °C), the Weather details warn "Ground frost likely" (-1) with the estimated ground temperature. The numbers are rule-of-thumb, not a measurement, and a forecast low already at or below 0 is covered by the plain low-temperature factor.
- **Noise** (`src/comfort/noise.ts`): the federal noise map's modelled night-time road and rail noise (BAFU sonBASE, dB(A) Lr night; the layers are rasters, read with a WMS GetFeatureInfo request) at the spot: 45 to 54 dB(A) is a warning (-1), 55 or more red (-2), 35 to 44 information, nothing modelled is stated without a score. Road and rail add up. It is modelled from traffic counts for the road and rail networks only, so aircraft, cableways, huts and livestock are not in it. *Cowbells*: a mapped alp within 500 m in June to September (the grazing season) costs one point, with the alp named; that is a heuristic from the alp's name and the season, not a record of animals.
- **Natural hazards** (`src/comfort/hazards.ts`): flood areas (Aquaprotect, 50 and 100 year events) and the SilvaProtect-CH indications for rockfall, landslide and debris flow, all FOEN national models. They are raster layers with no query service, so a 9 x 9 pixel map image of the spot is requested and its centre pixel read (coloured = inside the mapped area). A 50-year flood area costs 2 (red), a 100-year one 1; rockfall costs 1 only if a steep slope is close, else information; landslide and debris flow are information only, because their maps cover large parts of the mountains. These are coarse national models, not the cantonal hazard maps (Gefahrenkarten, which have no national service), and the text says that: a spot outside them is not guaranteed safe. The surface-runoff map was left out because its semi-transparent classes cover almost everything. Lightning is handled by the ridge, exposure and thunderstorm factors that already exist.
- **Snow and avalanche** (`src/comfort/avalanche.ts`): three parts, all counted with the weather. *Avalanche bulletin*: the SLF bulletin (CAAML as GeoJSON from aws.slf.ch, cached 30 minutes) is matched to the spot by its region polygon. The highest danger rating of the day is shown as a chip ("Avalanche danger: considerable (3)") and a detail with the problems, aspects and elevation bands; level 3 scores -1 (-2 with a slope of 25 degrees or more at the spot or a 30 degree slope above it within 150 m), level 4 and 5 score -2 (-4 near steep terrain), levels 1 and 2 are information only. A bulletin that has expired, or ends before the chosen night, does not count and says so. The SLF publishes only in the winter season: in summer nothing is current, which is stated (above 1800 m) and no chip is shown. The level describes the most dangerous slopes of a region, not your flat meadow, which is why the score depends on nearby steep terrain. *Snow depth*: the forecast model's snow depth at the spot's elevation (Open-Meteo `snow_depth`), minus 1 from 5 cm and minus 2 from 30 cm; a model value, not a measurement. *Steep-slope layer*: the Layers panel can show swisstopo's "slope over 30 degrees" overlay, where avalanches release (off by default). The bulletin parser was written against the SLF's archived bulletins of May 2026 (the endpoint was empty at the time of writing), so the live in-season response is expected to have the same shape but has not been seen.

### Safety and animals

- **On the first view**: the dangers that matter are lines under the overall score (three shown, "+N more in the details"): steep ground and drops, flood and rockfall areas, a storm or strong wind for the chosen night (and the hours before evening), avalanche danger, deep cold and snow, a fire ban or high fire danger, a pasture with working herd-protection dogs, army shooting today, no mobile signal, a flood warning of level 3 or more, and a ban zone that begins close by. Each line opens the details it comes from.
- **Fire and drones** (`src/restrictions.ts`): under the legality details, live official data for two things that are not camping rules and therefore do not change the verdict. *Fire*: BAFU's forest-fire danger for the region (`ch.bafu.gefahren-waldbrand_warnung`) and the measure the canton has in force (`ch.bafu.gefahren-waldbrand_praeventionsmassnahmen_kantone`), worded for the type of measure (no measure, a warning, a conditional ban such as "fires only at permanent fire places", a ban in and near forest, a ban on fires in the open). Every sentence is one an official source states (BAFU and the cantons of BE, GR, VS, TI and VD, `docs/sources/CH/README_fire.md`): camping stoves are named only in Bern's text, so the app never claims a stove is allowed, and says to ask the canton. *Drones*: BAZL's geographic UAS zones (`ch.bazl.einschraenkungen-drohnen`) with the exact restriction text and authority; a zone that forbids every drone (such as the Swiss National Park) comes first and red. Where nothing is listed the general rules are stated (120 m, line of sight, crowds, privacy) and that local nature rules may not be on the map. A failed lookup is shown as "could not be checked", never as "nothing applies".
- **Hunting and animals** (`src/wildlife.ts`, a tab under the legality details, closed at first; sources in `docs/sources/CH/README_hunting.md`, `README_herdenschutz.md`, `README_predators.md`): *Herd-protection dogs*: the federal layer of pastures with working dogs (`ch.bafu.alpweiden-mit_herdenschutzhunden`) within 500 m, with the provider's season (read as in, out or unsure of season: the texts say "as a rule", so two weeks either side count as unsure), its advice for walkers and a link to the official behaviour advice; inside a pasture in season it is a line on the first view. The layer's contact fields are personal data and are never shown. No official advice for camping next to guard dogs exists, and none is claimed. *Hunting*: the 2026 periods as the cantons published them (GR, VS, BE, TI, UR, GL, SZ, OW, NW, AI, AR, FR, VD, LU, ZG, JU, NE), with the date checked; hunters are out from before sunrise to after sunset in several cantons. *Wolves and bears* and *cattle on pastures*: the official advice (a tent is no protection, store food away from it, do not approach calves or mother cows). All of it is information and does not change the camping verdict.
- **Mobile coverage** (`src/comfort/coverage.ts`, `docs/sources/CH/README_mobile_coverage.md`): the operators' outdoor 4G and 5G prediction as BAKOM publishes it (`ch.bakom.mobilnetz-4g`, `-5g`, WMS images: the centre pixel of a 3 x 3 pixel picture, green = all three providers, orange = fewer, none = nothing predicted). No signal predicted is a warning line on the first view; a spot with signal gets a plain note. It is a prediction for outdoors, not a measurement: a hollow, forest or tent can still be a dead spot.
- **Flood warning** (`src/flood.ts`): the national warning of BAFU (`ch.bafu.hydroweb-warnkarte_national`, hazard level 1 to 5, read live by WMS GetFeatureInfo) for the spot's region, and for river sections and lakes where the map has them. Level 2 is a detail in the sleep tab; level 3 and up is also a line on the first view (and lowers the sleep score a little), level 4 and up red. The level names are those of the layer's own legend in the four languages (checked on the legend images). The advice is not to camp on a bank or shore or in a dry stream bed. It is live, so it is read for tonight only, not for a night weeks away.
- **Emergency** (`src/emergency.ts`, `src/emergencyview.ts`): the first entry of the map menu. The numbers (112, 144, 1414 Rega, 117) and what to say, from BAKOM, Rega and the SAC (`docs/sources/CH/README_emergency.md`), written in the app so the page needs no network; only your position comes from the phone, shown as WGS84 and Swiss coordinates for dictating to a dispatcher.
- **Close to a ban zone**: a federal ban zone that begins within about 150 m (a GPS fix and a map can be off by that much) adds "A ban zone begins about N m away" and turns "Likely OK" into "Caution".

### Planning

- **Best spots nearby** (`src/finder.ts`, `src/finderview.ts`): the "Find the best spots" button (on the start panel for the map centre, and under every result for that spot) scans about 700 m around a point, or 1.5 km (chips "How far to look", and an offer to look wider when little is found; when nothing suitable is found the nearest official campsites are named). One POST to the profile service reads a 23 x 23 elevation grid at 100 m; ground cover comes from four identify requests on the land-cover sample points, which lie on the same grid. Every node is scored with the same comfort rules as a full check (slope, wind shelter from the horizon, ridge or hollow, ground), steep (18 degrees or more over 200 m), glacier, water, built-up and wet nodes are dropped, and the best 12 at least 250 m apart go on. For those, legality (the full legality check) and water (the full water check, with glacier and sewage flags) are looked up four at a time; spots in banned areas are dropped and the rest are ranked half by legality score and half by sleep score, five shown, numbered on the map. Tapping a row opens that spot's full check, with a link back to the list. It is a screening: the elevation grid is coarser than the 10 m profile of a full check (so no steep-drop or steep-slope-above hazards), crowds and weather are not in the ranking, and ground is read from the nearest sample point. Each row shows the walking time (Naismith's rule, optimistic on rough ground), and a line above the list gives tonight's weather around the place. Typical time is 10 to 15 s.
- **Saved spots** (`src/saved.ts`, `src/savedview.ts`): "Save this spot" stores a spot in this browser (localStorage, up to 200, one per place) with the scores it had at that moment and a name and a note you can change. The star button in the map menu lists them with a sort (newest, name, nearest to the map centre or you, best overall), "Show all on the map" (the spots also appear as small stars on the map, switchable in Settings), "Check the legality of all again" (judged again for today, listing what changed) and "Import spots from a GPX file". Opening one starts a fresh check; ticking 2 to 4 compares them side by side with the best score per row highlighted. Removing a spot asks once. Nothing leaves the device unless you share it; clearing site data or private mode loses the list.
- **Trip planner** (`src/trip.ts`, `src/tripview.ts`, `src/planner.ts`, `src/planview.ts`): a trip is dated nights (up to 21, up to a year ahead), each with a saved spot (a spot can repeat). Each night's legality is judged again for its own date (quiet-zone seasons, shooting days), from the spot's lookups made once; the weather score comes from a fresh forecast at the spot's elevation for nights the forecast reaches (16 days; more than a week ahead is marked a rough guide), and a night beyond it says so instead of guessing. Flags: thunderstorm, storm gusts, strong wind, heavy rain, new snow, frost. The weakest night is named only when it is below 70 and differs from the rest; a night the weather alone rules out says so. A spot whose forecast failed is not counted as fine. The page prints (coordinates shown) and "Save the maps" stores the map around the spots for offline use. An older trip (spot order only) is migrated.
- **Route** (`src/routecheck.ts`, `src/routeplan.ts`, `src/routeview.ts`): the map menu's Route takes a GPX file (a file with one track per day is one route when the tracks connect within 3 km; otherwise the longest track) and draws it. The whole line is checked in one pass for each day's date: the federal zones it crosses (asked for per 30 km piece with their outlines, so the stretches inside them are placed to a few metres), the municipalities with a recorded rule, the cantons, the bundled reserves and the forest and treeline, with the same `assess` as a tapped spot, so the two never disagree. A strip shows ok, caution and ban along the distance, with the stretches listed (tap one to centre it) and the municipal rules met. Length, climb and walking time (Naismith) come from the file's own heights or from the elevation service. The route is cut into days (5 to 40 km each, 15 by default); each day's camp is the end of the stage, or the nearest place along the route that is not banned within 3 km (it says when everything within reach is banned, and then names the nearest official campsites). "Plan the nights with these camps" saves the camps as spots and fills a trip; "Download the route with the camps" writes a GPX; "Save the map along the route" stores tiles within 1.2 km of the line for offline use. The route is kept in this browser (simplified to 3,000 points). Not checked along a route: settlements, huts and inns, hunting and the Jura reserves, and the page says so; a route outside Switzerland says that nothing can be said about it.
- **Share links** (`src/share.ts`): a link carrying places with their names and notes, and optionally a dated trip, packed into the part after `#share=` (up to 40 places). Nothing is sent anywhere: the address never leaves the two phones. No scores travel with it; whoever opens it sees the places and checks them for themselves with today's rules and forecast, and can add them to their list. A link that is damaged or places outside Switzerland are refused.
- **Share and export** (`src/gpx.ts`): "Share this spot" under a result shares the place, the three scores (sleep marked partial if inputs were still arriving), the verdict and the link through the phone's share sheet, or copies the same text where there is none. "Download as GPX" saves the spot, and the saved list has "Export all as GPX": GPX 1.1 waypoints with name, elevation, the save time and a description with the scores, water and hut lines, points for and against and the "not legal advice" note, ready for a navigation app. Scores in the file are as of the save date.
- **Search by coordinates** (`src/coordsearch.ts`): the search box also takes coordinates (WGS84 in decimal degrees or degrees-minutes-seconds, Swiss LV95 and LV03, and links from map apps), and says what it could not read.

### Where instead, and how you sleep

- **Official campsites** (`src/campsites.ts`, `scripts/build_campsites.py`, `public/campsites.json.gz`, 8 KB): swisstopo's swissNAMES3D objects "Campingplatzareal" (campsite area) and "Standplatzareal" (area for caravans and bungalows with permanent use), 894 polygons merged into 494 points (426 campsites, 68 caravan-only areas), bundled and cached for offline use. A spot that is "Not allowed" or "Caution" shows the three nearest on the first view with their distances (to the middle of the area); an empty best-spots search and a route day with nothing within reach name them too. The data says that an area is mapped there, not whether it is open, takes tents or what it costs, and the line says so. Rebuild with `python3 scripts/build_campsites.py`.
- **How you sleep** (`src/gear.ts`, in Settings): the shelter (tent, tarp or hammock, bivy bag or under the stars, car, van or motorhome), how many people and whether a dog comes along, kept in this browser. The rules the app holds are written for tents, so for another set-up the legality details start with notes on how the recorded municipal and cantonal texts read for it: tent-less sleeping counted as camping (Brienz), left out or allowed under conditions (Kandersteg, Biel, Thun), or not mentioned; tarps and emergency roofs named like tents (Interlaken, Spiez, Thun); vehicles named; groups named (NW, SG). The classification is read from the wording of the recorded summaries and pinned by a test for every municipality. The notes only quote what the texts say, never make a spot legal, and say plainly when the app has no source (for example for sleeping in a vehicle). The set-up shows as a line under the place when it is not a tent for one.

### Offline, installing and privacy

- **Offline use** (`public/sw.js`, `src/offline.ts`): in the deployed build a service worker keeps the app, its bundled data (forest map, treeline surface, reserve and ban polygons, campsites) and every map tile or overlay you viewed, so the app opens and the map shows without a connection. What works offline: the map, the forest and treeline check, the bundled reserve and ban polygons, saved spots with the scores they had, the emergency page. What does not: zone, canton, municipality, water and shelter lookups, search and the forecast (an orange banner says so, and a new spot's legality lists the checks it could not make). Lookups you made before are served from a saved copy only when the network fails; the forecast and search are never cached, so an old forecast is never shown as current. Overlays appear offline only where you viewed them online.
- **Saving maps on purpose**: tiles you save go into their own cache that is never trimmed (browsing tiles are trimmed to 6,000). "Save map for offline" in the map menu saves the visible area (zoom from the current one up to 15, up to 500 tiles, about 15 MB). A route has "Save the map along the route" (1.2 km each side, zoom 12 up to 16, as many levels as fit 3,000 tiles) and saved spots and trips have "Save the map around these spots" (1 km around each, zoom 13 up to 16); a download of more than 500 tiles says its size and starts on a second tap, a second tap while it runs stops it, and the total is capped at 6,000 tiles (about 180 MB). The browser is asked to keep the storage. Settings lists what is saved and offers to delete it.
- **Installing and updating**: a web app manifest and icons make the app installable (an "Install the app" button in Settings where the browser offers it, otherwise a hint). When a newer version has been downloaded in the background the app says so with a "Reload" button (the cached page is compared with the script that is running, so a changed comment does not trigger it). Caches of older versions are removed when the new worker starts, files only an old version needed are dropped once nothing asks for them, and the bundled data files are checked against the server once a day and replaced when they changed.
- **Privacy** (`src/privacy.ts`, `src/settingsview.ts`, "Your data and privacy" in Settings): says what is kept on the device (saved spots, trips, the route, language, set-up, notes about saved maps; nothing is uploaded) and what is sent when a spot is checked or the map moves (swisstopo and the federal offices for the spot, zones and the map; Open-Meteo for the forecast at about ten metres; the SLF bulletin is downloaded whole and carries nothing about you). No accounts, advertising, analytics or cookies. The phone's position is read only when you tap the location button, once, and the receiver is switched off again. Two buttons delete your spots, trips and route, or everything the app stored (each asks twice). Tests check that every host the source talks to is listed and that every storage key the source writes is covered by "delete everything".

### Language and accessibility

- **Languages** (`src/i18n.ts`, `src/i18n-dict.ts`, `src/i18n-rules.ts`): German, French and Italian next to English, chosen in Settings (the browser's language is the default; the choice is kept and the page reloads). The English text is the translation key, so anything not translated shows in English instead of breaking. Translated: the interface, the notes and findings, and the summaries of cantonal and municipal rules; quotations from the laws stay in their original language. A test fails if a text passed to `tr()` or marked `data-i18n` has no translation in any of the three languages, or if a translation drops a placeholder. The translations are mine, not reviewed by a native speaker; wording corrections are welcome as issues.
- **Accessibility** (`src/a11y.ts`): the map takes keyboard focus (arrow keys move it, plus and minus zoom); Enter checks the spot in the middle, where a cross shows while the map has focus, and Escape closes the menu, a panel or the sheet. The sheet is not a live region (it changes many times during a check); a hidden status region announces when a check finishes ("Bergün Filisur. Likely OK. Overall 60 out of 100."), when the best spots are found and when a route is checked. Every detail carries a sign and a word (Problem, Caution, Fine, Note) as well as its edge colour. Regions and panels are named, a request for less motion is respected (no gliding or fading map, no animations), and the page passes the axe-core checks (WCAG 2.2 AA and best practice) on every page in light and dark.

### Interface and speed

- **Result sheet, kept calm** (after user feedback that it was overwhelming): the first view used to show 3 score cards, 4 chips, 5 buttons and a disclaimer (13 buttons, more than the sheet could hold). Now it shows the place, one large legality answer with a one-line reason, the Sleep and Weather scores beside each other, two buttons (Save, Best spots) and a collapsed "More options" (share, copy link, GPX, report a rule). Everything else is one tap away: opening a card shrinks the three cards to a row of tabs that stay in view; water and hut sit under the sleep summary, the avalanche line at the top of the weather details; only the first three findings of a section are listed, with "Show all"; and the scoring explanation is collapsed under "How is this scored?". The first view now fits the sheet without scrolling.
- **One overall score first** tapping a spot shows a single score (half legality, half the spot's comfort; the weather is left out so places can be judged in advance; a ban makes it 0 and "caution" is never called good) with a one-line reason and "View details". That opens the Legality, Sleep and Weather cards, with "← Overall score" to go back.
- **Start panel, simplified** it used to carry a heading, two paragraphs, two buttons and a disclaimer (70 words, 39 % of the screen). It now has the heading, one line ("Tap the map to check a spot, or search for a place."), one button (Best spots in this area) and a one-line "Guidance only, not legal advice." that opens the rest of the disclaimer (28 words, 23 % of the screen). The saved-spots button was dropped from it because the star button on the map opens the same list.
- **Map menu and settings**: the map's actions are in one menu button under the zoom control: Emergency, My location, Saved spots, Trip planner, Route, Save map for offline, Map layers, Settings. Settings holds the language, how you sleep, offline maps and installing, and your data and privacy; Map layers holds the trails, steep-slope, saved-spot and restricted-zone toggles. Both panels stay above the sheet, and the menu does too, however tall the sheet is.
- **Speed** a tap runs the legality lookups at once and shows the legality card as soon as they are in (about 1 to 3 s). The sleep score then appears as soon as any part of its data has arrived, marked with "…" and a "still checking" note, and refreshes as terrain, trails and huts, water, ground cover, the avalanche bulletin, noise, natural hazards and the forecast come in. Every request has a time limit (7 s for legality lookups, 6 s per sleep part) and the whole check has a budget of about 10 s from the tap: anything still missing then is dropped and listed as not checked, never counted as good. The very first tap also waits (up to 6 s) for the local map data (forest map 3.7 MB, reserves) to download.
- **Map loading** the first screen asks for as little as possible. The map's own tiles load first; the local data (3.7 MB forest map, reserves, treeline surface) is fetched once the first view's tiles are in, or at once when a check needs it. The restricted-zone, trail and slope overlays start only at the zooms where they say something (zones from zoom 9, trails and slope from 11) and use 512 px tiles, a quarter of the requests of the 256 px default, so the national start view loads the base map only. Tiles load when a pan or zoom settles instead of during it, and only one ring of off-screen tiles is kept. The page opens early connections to the swisstopo hosts, and the offline cache serves the bundled data files without re-downloading them on every visit (they used to be revalidated in the background each time).
- **Missing map tiles** a tile whose request failed (a dropped connection, a busy tile server) used to stay a grey hole until the next pan, which is the staircase of missing squares. Now the base map and the overlays ask for a failed tile again after 0.5 s, 1 s and 2 s (`src/tilelayer.ts`, not while offline), and the offline service worker retries once and then passes the plain image request through instead of failing. Its cache trimming, which scanned the whole tile cache after every tile and so slowed every response, now runs once per 200 stored tiles, and tiles are answered before they are written to the cache. With 30 % of first tile requests failing, the old code showed 25 of 35 tiles (71 %) and the new code 35 of 35, also with the service worker active.
- **Loading order and weight** (1) the HTML itself starts fetching the first view's base tiles before the JavaScript has loaded, using the same tile maths as the map (for a deep link, from the link's own position and zoom); in a browser check the preloaded tiles matched the visible ones exactly (8 of 8, 24 of 24, 15 of 15) with none fetched twice. (2) The zone and trail overlays, the heaviest requests, are added only after the base tiles of the first view are in (or after 2.5 s), and respect the layer checkboxes. (3) Overlay images are 8-bit PNG: the same picture (0 to 7 pixels differ out of tens of thousands) at 45 % fewer bytes, for example a trails tile 101 KB to 55 KB. (4) The 3.7 MB forest map and the other local data are fetched at low priority once the map has drawn, and normal priority only when a check is waiting for them. (5) Preconnects cover both the plain-image and the CORS connection to the tile server. Measured and rejected: decoding the local data costs next to nothing (it is a typed-array view), so a web worker would not help; the server returns WebP only as an error image, so 8-bit PNG is the smallest overlay format available.
- **No lazily loaded code** the finder and planner code was briefly split into separate files loaded on first use. With the offline service worker (which serves the cached page while it updates) that can pair an old entry file with a newer chunk and make the feature fail silently, so the app is a single bundle again; a test fails if any `import()` is added.

## Limits
There is no federal wild-camping law. Rules are cantonal and municipal, and the treeline is a rule of thumb, not a legal test. Private land, grazing areas, and local bans are not checked. Hunting, herd-protection dogs, fire and flood notices are information from official sources and do not change the camping verdict; a green result is never a permission.

## Status
Verified against the live geo.admin.ch API:
- All zone layer IDs exist and are queryable via `identify`. The dedicated National Park layer is *not* queryable, so the parks layer is used and filtered to category `SNP`.
- Zone names come from each feature's `label`; wildlife quiet zones also report the restriction, protection season and canton.
- Elevation via the `height` service.

Forest map accuracy (checked against the source polygons): 294/300 points inside large forests read as forest; 295/300 points 60 m+ from any large forest read as not-forest (misses are small, open, or shrub forest). Grid resolution is 25 m, so edges are approximate. Forest data is swissTLM3D 2026-02; rebuild to refresh.

Also verified: the API sends `access-control-allow-origin: *`, and the built app runs in headless Chromium (forest map download/decompression, lookup, rendering, the service worker offline, the update notice, route import and checking against the live layers, map downloads, keyboard use and axe-core).

## Rebuilding the data
```
pip install -r scripts/requirements.txt
# forest and treeline: download swissTLM3D (shapefile) from https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.swisstopo.swisstlm3d/ and extract TLM_BB/
python3 scripts/build_forest_mask.py <dir-with-BODENBEDECKUNG-shp> public/forest-mask.bin.gz 25
python3 scripts/build_treeline_surface.py <dir-with-BODENBEDECKUNG-shp> public/treeline-surface.bin.gz
# official campsites (one request, a few seconds)
python3 scripts/build_campsites.py public/campsites.json.gz
```
The full swissTLM3D download is about 3.6 GB; only the land-cover files are used. Takes ~1 minute. The reserve files are built by `scripts/build_*_reserves.py`.
