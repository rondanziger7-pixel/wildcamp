## Bottom line (draft app text, 6 sentences)
Switzerland has no single rule on sleeping in a vehicle: road traffic law decides where and how long you may park, while camping is left to the cantons and municipalities, and they differ. Obwalden expressly allows one night in a caravan or motorhome outside a campsite without a permit if no public or private interest is impaired, whereas Ticino and municipalities such as Interlaken, Arosa and Locarno allow overnighting in vehicles only at places the authorities designate. Some towns (Thun, Spiez) allow a single night in a camper or caravan on a public car park unless a sign says otherwise, and Fribourg's city regulation only requires a permit above 24 hours. In most cantons we found no explicit rule on sleeping in a car or van, so check the local police regulation, the signs at the car park and the landowner's wishes; Appenzell Innerrhoden has announced a ban on wild camping on public and private car parks. Never drive onto forest roads or into nature reserves to sleep. If you are too tired to drive, stopping is safer than driving on, but only a few rules (Arosa) say an unforeseen emergency night in the vehicle is not camping, so do not count on an exemption.

## Federal rules
What is federal and what is not (plain version):
- Federal: road traffic law (SVG and its ordinances VRV, SSV) governs parking on public roads - where, signs, maximum duration, prohibitions. It does not (as far as I could establish; UNVERIFIED) contain a rule on sleeping in a parked vehicle, and there is no federal "camping zone" rule. A secondary source (TCS, via search summary) says the same: "Eine nationale Regelung betreffend Übernachten im Wohnmobil auf Raststätten, Parkplätzen oder anderen öffentlichen Plätzen gibt es in der Schweiz nicht" (UNVERIFIED, secondary, could not open page).
- Cantonal/municipal: whether overnighting in a vehicle is "camping", where it is allowed, permits, fines (police regulations, camping laws, planning law). Local traffic orders (signs such as no-parking, time limits) are issued by cantonal/municipal authorities under federal road traffic law.
- Federal forest law: motor vehicles in forests and on forest roads.

## Driver rest (question 3)
- No Swiss federal or cantonal provision found that treats a driver's rest ("Ruhepause") as legally different from camping. Federal fitness-to-drive law (SVG Art. 31 para. 2, UNVERIFIED) tells you not to drive when over-tired but is not an exemption from local camping/parking rules. (Germany has a case-law concept "Wiederherstellung der Fahrtüchtigkeit"; do NOT assume Switzerland has one.)
- One municipal rule found: Arosa Reglement Art. 11 (quoted above) - emergency exemption for unforeseen fatigue. Locarno has designated 24 h emergency areas for motorhomes (Art. 3). Interlaken's wording ("Übernachten in Fahrzeugen aller Art ... nur an den von den Behörden bezeichneten Stellen") has no exemption.
- Driver-hours law for professional drivers (ARV) not researched and not relevant to private campers.
